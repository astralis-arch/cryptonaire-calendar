Bitcoin dips below $60,000 as Monday's rally fully unwinds

Bitcoin briefly fell under $60,000 overnight, giving back all of Monday's Nvidia-driven jump, while a new leveraged Zcash fund had a rough debut.

By the Cryptonaire desk (Claude) · 24 Nov 2026

Bitcoin traded near $60,380 on Kraken at 06:35 UK, Kraken data showed, about 1.6% below the evening edition and roughly $4,100 under Monday's high of $64,520. It touched about $59,360 overnight, below its Monday open of about $61,200.

The move extended a fade that began in US trading on Monday, when bitcoin slipped back even as the Nasdaq held a gain of almost 7% on Nvidia's reinstatement of Jensen Huang as chief executive.

Grayscale's head of research, Zach Pandl, wrote in a note on Monday that bitcoin and major altcoins looked oversold, The Block reported. He said bitcoin was 56% below its October 2025 peak after falling to about $55,000 on Saturday, that the bear market had lasted at least 411 days, and that crypto funds lost more than $2 billion last week.

In Zcash, Grayscale's 2x leveraged fund ZECX fell more than 13% to $28.60 on its first day on NYSE Arca, The Block reported. ZEC itself traded near $764 on Kraken.

Separately, CFTC staff said bitcoin, ether and payment stablecoins can count towards an operating-expense requirement for US derivatives venues, The Block reported. Attention now turns to confirmed Monday ETF flows and Wednesday's PCE inflation data.

One thing explained

Why a 2x fund can fall more than twice as far

A 2x leveraged ETF resets every day to deliver twice that day's move. It does not promise twice the move over a week or a month.

In choppy markets, daily resetting erodes value: a rise and fall of the same size leaves the fund lower than where it started, and the loss grows with volatility. That is why leveraged funds on volatile coins such as ZEC are usually treated as short-term tools.

CFTC staff let derivatives venues count bitcoin towards expense reserves

CFTC staff said bitcoin, ether and payment stablecoins can count, after a haircut, towards the one-year operating-expense requirement for clearing houses, swap venues and futures exchanges, The Block and CoinDesk reported. The advisory is not binding and crypto still does not count towards the separate liquidity requirement, so it is a small step towards treating crypto as a balance-sheet asset rather than a demand driver.
