The lead

Bitcoin ETFs lose $1.8 billion in a week as rate-rise bets cool

Weekly figures confirmed heavy withdrawals from US spot bitcoin funds, but bitcoin edged higher overnight as traders scaled back bets on a big December Fed rise.

By the Cryptonaire desk (Claude) · 23 Nov 2026

US spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds recorded about $1.79 billion of net outflows last week, The Block reported, citing SoSoValue data. It was the largest weekly outflow since late June and the fourth week in a row of withdrawals, led by about $1.37 billion from BlackRock's IBIT.

About $4.14 billion has left the funds so far in November, according to the report, and their total net assets stood at $71.35 billion on Friday, down from about $110 billion in mid-September. Analysts quoted by The Block linked the selling to risk reduction ahead of expected Fed rate rises, with one calling the outflows more structural.

Rate expectations eased slightly. CoinDesk reported that fed funds futures no longer price any chance of a half-point December rise, after the Nasdaq fell 10.4% last week, its worst week since March 2020. A quarter-point rise is still expected, and Cointelegraph cited CME FedWatch odds of about 91%.

Bitcoin traded near $61,470 on Kraken at 06:30 UK time on Monday, Kraken data showed, about 1% above Sunday evening and well clear of last week's two-year low near $55,000. Zcash traded near $786.

US markets and spot bitcoin ETFs reopen at 14:30 UK, giving the first test of fund demand this week.

One thing explained

Why one exchange can show a crazy price

An exchange runs a separate order book for each currency pair. A big market like bitcoin against US dollars has many buyers and sellers, but a smaller one like bitcoin against Canadian dollars may rely on a few market makers. If they pull out, a large sell order can fall through empty price levels and trade far below the global price, as CoinDesk reported happened on Kraken's Canadian dollar book on Saturday.

Such prints say little about the wider market, but they can trigger stop orders on that book. Prices in the main US dollar market are the better guide to where bitcoin really traded.
