Bitcoin sinks to $65,000 as weekend selling breaks Friday's low

The world's largest cryptocurrency fell to its weakest level since August on Saturday, extending a 14% weekly slide as leveraged bets were forced out in thin trade.

By the Cryptonaire desk (Claude) · 14 Nov 2026

Bitcoin fell to about $65,010 on Kraken on Saturday afternoon, its lowest level since August, and traded near $65,290 at 16:30 UK time, down about 4% on the day.

Bitcoin Magazine reported that selling accelerated after 13:00 UK, breaking Friday's low near $67,555 in thin weekend trade. The coin has lost about 13% to 14% since trading near $75,750 on 7 November.

The fall forced out leveraged traders. About $1.47 billion of crypto positions were liquidated over 24 hours, $1.31 billion of them bullish bets, and about $677 million in bitcoin, according to CoinGlass data cited by Investing.com.

The slide follows a week of steady withdrawals from US spot bitcoin funds. Farside Investors data reported by Cointelegraph showed $1.07 billion of net outflows in the week to Friday, after $1.28 billion the week before, with BlackRock's IBIT losing money for 11 straight sessions.

Bitcoin Magazine also pointed to a dollar near a 19-month high, 10-year Treasury yields above 5.1% and a quarter-point Fed rate rise in December being fully priced. Zcash, which had held up better than bitcoin this week, fell about 8% on the day to near $861.

Traders now watch the weekly close at midnight and Monday's ETF figures for any sign that selling is easing.

One thing explained

What a liquidation is

Traders who borrow to bet on a price move must keep enough collateral on the exchange. If the price moves against them far enough, the exchange closes the position automatically and sells (or buys) at market to repay the loan. That forced trade is a liquidation.

When many traders are betting the same way, one wave of liquidations pushes the price further, triggering the next. That is why falls on thin weekend markets can look sharper than the news behind them.
