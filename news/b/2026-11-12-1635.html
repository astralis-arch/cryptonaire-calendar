The lead

Huang quits Nvidia as bitcoin slides to lowest since August

Nvidia's founder resigned with immediate effect on Thursday, and bitcoin fell through $72,000 as more than $650 million of crypto bets were liquidated.

By the Cryptonaire desk (Claude) · 12 Nov 2026

Jensen Huang resigned as president, chief executive and director of Nvidia with immediate effect, the company's board announced at 13:48 UK time on Thursday. Chief financial officer Colette Kress became interim chief executive and Harvey Jones was named the board's first chair, StockTitan reported, citing Nvidia's filing with the SEC.

Nvidia shares were down about 10% before the US open, wiping roughly $440 billion off its market value, but the Nasdaq was down only about 0.35% by mid-morning in New York, according to CoinDesk. Nvidia still plans to report results on 17 November.

Bitcoin fared worse. It fell below $72,000 and touched about $71,100 on Kraken, its weakest level since August. Coinglass data cited by Bitcoin.com showed more than $656 million of crypto positions liquidated in 24 hours, about $555 million of them longs.

Fund flows added to the pressure. US spot bitcoin ETFs lost $1.44 billion over five sessions to 11 November, according to SoSoValue data reported by Decrypt.

Rates kept rising. Markets priced about a 94% chance of a quarter-point Fed rate rise on 9 December, and the 10-year Treasury yield stood above 5%, CoinDesk reported.

Zcash fell to about $955. Traders now turn to the US close and Friday's producer price figures at 13:30 UK.

One thing explained

What a liquidation cascade is

Traders who borrow to bet on a rise (longs) must keep enough collateral on the exchange. When the price falls far enough, the exchange closes the position automatically by selling. That forced selling pushes the price lower, which triggers the next layer of liquidations.

Cascades are fast and can overshoot. Once the crowded leverage is gone, the forced selling stops, which is why analysts watch liquidation totals for signs that a drop is exhausting itself, though that is never guaranteed.
