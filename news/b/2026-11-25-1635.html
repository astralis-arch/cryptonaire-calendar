The lead

Hot inflation and tariff threat sink Nasdaq 4%, dragging bitcoin lower

A firmer-than-expected US inflation reading and new tariff proposals hit technology shares at the open, and bitcoin briefly fell to about $57,200 before steadying.

By the Cryptonaire desk (Claude) · 25 Nov 2026

The Nasdaq Composite fell about 4% early in Wednesday's US session, with the S&P 500 down about 3%, Bitcoin Magazine reported. Nvidia sank more than 8%, and Micron and AMD each fell more than 9%.

The selling followed October's PCE figures, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge. Core PCE rose to 3.3% a year, above the 3.2% forecast, while headline PCE was 3.8%, CNBC and Bitcoin Magazine reported. Third-quarter growth was revised up to 4.3%.

Separately, the US proposed new tariffs on 14 trading partners including China, the EU and Japan, Euronews reported. The UK is not on the list, and a comment period could delay any duties until January.

Bitcoin fell to about $57,210 on Kraken shortly after the open, below the $58,500 level Glassnode had flagged, before recovering to near $58,540 by 16:30 UK. Zcash fell harder, with ZEC down about 6.5% since the morning to near $688.

Fund selling continued. US spot bitcoin ETFs lost $231.6 million on Tuesday, according to SoSoValue data reported by Cointelegraph, taking November's outflows to a record $4.94 billion.

Markets already price a near-certain Fed rate rise on 9 December, Bitcoin Magazine reported, and US markets close on Thursday for Thanksgiving.

Corrections: This morning's edition said US spot bitcoin ETFs lost $570 million on Tuesday, citing The Block. SoSoValue and Farside data, as reported by Cointelegraph, put Tuesday's outflow at about $231.5 million.

One thing explained

Headline versus core inflation

Headline inflation counts everything people buy. Core inflation leaves out food and energy, which swing about with weather and oil prices, so it gives a steadier view of the underlying trend.

Central banks such as the Fed watch core most closely. That is why a core reading just a tenth of a point above forecast, as today, can move markets more than the headline number.
