The lead

Hot US inflation puts a bigger Fed rate rise on the table

Prices rose faster than expected in October. Bitcoin touched an 11-week low before steadying, while Wall Street gave back part of Monday's rally.

By the Cryptonaire desk (Claude) · 10 Nov 2026

US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, above the 0.3% economists had forecast, the Bureau of Labor Statistics said on Tuesday. Core prices, which strip out food and energy, rose 0.3% against forecasts of 0.2%, CoinDesk reported.

Food rose 0.5% and energy 0.7%, while owners' equivalent rent climbed 0.3%, according to Yahoo Finance. There was again no official annual figure, because last year's government shutdown left a gap in the data.

The report mattered because of what Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller said earlier in the day. He backed a quarter-point rise in December even if inflation cooled, and said a half-point move should be considered if core prices rose 0.3% or more, TheStreet reported. Core landed exactly on that line.

Markets moved quickly. The two-year Treasury yield rose 14 basis points to 4.69% and the 10-year reached 4.93%, CoinDesk reported. CME FedWatch put the chance of a half-point move at roughly 15%, with a quarter-point rise the rest.

Bitcoin fell to about $73,400 after the release, its weakest level since 21 August, Bitcoin Magazine and KuCoin reported, before recovering above $74,000. Spot bitcoin funds had already lost $201m on Monday, the seventh straight session of withdrawals from BlackRock's IBIT.

The S&P 500 was down about 0.9% and the Nasdaq 1.3% at midday in New York, according to 24/7 Wall St. Producer prices follow on Friday.

In brief

October CPI ran hot (0.4%, core 0.3%), making a December Fed hike look certain and a half-point move possible. Bitcoin touched an 11-week low near $73.4k before steadying above $74k.

One thing explained

Why analysts talk about a "daily close"

Crypto trades around the clock, so there is no closing bell. By convention the daily candle closes at midnight UTC, which is midnight in the UK in winter.

Analysts watch where the price sits at that moment because brief dips below a level often come from thin trading or forced liquidations and bounce straight back. A close below the level shows it failed to hold through the whole day. It is a convention, not a rule, and venues can differ by a few dollars.
