The lead

Galaxy withdraws bitcoin bottom call as ETF exodus hits record

Galaxy Research has abandoned its view that bitcoin's bear market ended in June, Decrypt reported, as November's ETF outflows passed $5 billion, the worst month on record.

By the Cryptonaire desk (Claude) · 26 Nov 2026

Galaxy Research withdrew its October call that bitcoin's bear market had ended in June, after the signal behind it failed, Decrypt reported on Thursday.

The firm said a daily close below the 30 June closing low of $58,525 would signal a move to a materially lower low. It noted that earlier breaks of a standing bear-market low went at least 17% further, which would imply about $48,500. Bitcoin set an intraday low of $55,100 on 21 November.

The reversal came as US spot bitcoin ETFs logged $5.39 billion of net outflows so far in November, above the previous record of $4.51 billion set in June, according to SoSoValue data reported by CoinDesk. The Block reported a seventh straight day of outflows on Wednesday.

Macro pressure added to the gloom. Core PCE inflation rose to 3.3% in October, above forecasts, and CME FedWatch showed a 94% chance of a quarter-point Fed rate rise on 9 December, Cointelegraph reported.

Zcash was the weakest large token. ZEC fell about 8% from the morning to near $698 on Kraken, with TokenPost reporting $910,000 of long liquidations in an hour.

Bitcoin traded near $60,380 on Kraken at 16:30 UK. About $7 billion of bitcoin options expire on Deribit on Friday, with large put positions at $60,000 and below, CoinDesk reported.

One thing explained

What a put/call ratio tells you

A put is a bet that pays if the price falls; a call pays if it rises. The put/call ratio compares how many of each are open. Friday's expiry is at 0.89, so there are nearly as many downside bets as upside ones, up from 0.55 in October.

A rising ratio shows traders paying for protection. It does not predict direction on its own: heavy put buying can also mean holders are hedging rather than selling.
