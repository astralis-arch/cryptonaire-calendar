Bitcoin slips under $59,000 as ETF outflows near $5 billion

Bitcoin drifted lower through the Asian session, nearing a level analysts flagged as a gateway to deeper losses, as fund outflows and a miner's selling plans added to the pressure.

By the Cryptonaire desk (Claude) · 25 Nov 2026

Bitcoin fell below $59,000 overnight, trading near $58,970 on Kraken at 06:30 UK after a low of about $58,860, down from roughly $59,870 at Tuesday evening's close of the US session.

US spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds recorded another $570 million of net outflows on Tuesday, The Block reported, bringing November's total close to $5 billion. The figure could not be checked against a second flow tracker this morning.

Separately, miner CleanSpark reported a net loss of $987 million for its fiscal year to September, The Block reported, with about two thirds of that from the falling value of its bitcoin. Its chief financial officer said on Tuesday's earnings call that the company may sell slightly more bitcoin than it mines each quarter to reduce risk, according to Cointelegraph. It held about 13,500 bitcoin at the end of September.

On Tuesday, Decrypt, citing Glassnode, reported that a close below $58,500 could open a path towards bitcoin's realised price near $53,200.

Attention now turns to US PCE inflation and third-quarter growth figures at 13:30 UK, the last major data before US markets close on Thursday for Thanksgiving. Zcash was steady, with ZEC near $736.

One thing explained

Why miners sell bitcoin

Miners earn new bitcoin but pay their electricity, staff and debts in dollars, so most sell some of what they mine. When the price falls, each coin covers less of those costs, and some sell more, dipping into coins they had saved.

A miner selling more than it mines turns from a holder into a net seller. For one firm the amounts are small, but if many do it at once it adds steady supply to the market.
