Bitcoin dips near $62,000 overnight, then snaps back

A further wave of forced selling in Asian trade took bitcoin to its weakest level since July before buyers pushed it back above $64,000, with Zcash rebounding harder still.

By the Cryptonaire desk (Claude) · 15 Nov 2026

Bitcoin fell to about $61,970 on Kraken between 01:00 and 02:00 UK time on Sunday, extending the weekend sell-off, before rebounding to about $65,590 by 03:00. It traded near $64,470 at 06:30 UK.

Bloomberg reported the low as its weakest level since 14 July, after a 5.7% fall during Saturday's US hours and a further 3.6% in Asia. About $2 billion of leveraged positions were liquidated across exchanges in the 24 hours to 9am Singapore time, including $774 million in bitcoin and $488 million in ether, Bloomberg said, citing Coinglass.

GSR co-founder Rich Rosenblum described the slide below the 200-day moving average, near $73,000, as a technical move, according to Bloomberg. The report also pointed to expectations of higher US interest rates and almost $3 billion of outflows from US spot bitcoin ETFs between 30 October and 13 November.

Zcash moved more sharply in the other direction. After touching about $840 on Kraken overnight, it jumped to about $931 shortly after 02:00 UK and traded near $894, up about 8% since Saturday evening.

Bitcoin remains about 25% below its 21 October level, Bloomberg reported. Its 2026 low is about $57,700, set on 1 July. Attention turns to the weekly close at midnight UK tonight and Monday's ETF flow figures.

Corrections: The evening edition of 14 November said the bitcoin weekly close was at midnight UK that night. The weekly candle closes at midnight UK on Sunday night, tonight.

One thing explained

What a liquidation cascade is

Traders who borrow to bet on a rising price must keep enough collateral on the exchange. When the price falls far enough, the exchange closes the position automatically by selling, which is a liquidation.

Those forced sales push the price lower, which triggers the next layer of liquidations, and so on. Once most of the borrowed bets are gone, the selling pressure can vanish quickly, which is why sharp drops like last night's are often followed by fast rebounds.
