Bitcoin stalls below $60,000 as stablecoin supply shrinks

A quiet evening after the US-open sell-off left bitcoin about 1% down on the day, as CryptoQuant warned that falling Tether supply has never coincided with a record high.

By the Cryptonaire desk (Claude) · 27 Nov 2026

Bitcoin traded near $59,750 at 21:30 UK on Friday, according to Kraken data, about 1.2% below the day's open of $60,498. It held a narrow range of roughly $59,450 to $60,000 after US stock markets closed early at 18:00 UK.

The calm followed a sharp drop at the US open, when bitcoin fell from about $62,100 to $58,700 and $228 million of crypto positions were liquidated in an hour, Cointelegraph reported, citing CoinGlass data.

CryptoQuant said the supply of the Tether stablecoin on Ethereum and Tron fell $8.1 billion, or 4.4%, in the 60 days to 23 November, the biggest such fall since August 2022, The Block reported. Bitcoin has not closed at a record high during any period of falling USDT supply since 2022, the firm said, though part of the fall reflected funds moving between chains.

Separately, CryptoQuant data showed large holders with 1,000 to 10,000 BTC added about 121,000 BTC between 30 September and 21 November despite a fall of nearly 30% in price, Cointelegraph reported.

Binance said its withdrawal suspension after last month's $6.3 billion hack was fully lifted, Bitcoin Magazine reported. Zcash held near $737, about 8% up on the day. The daily close comes at midnight UK, with Galaxy's $58,525 warning level about $1,200 below the price.

One thing explained

Why stablecoin supply matters

Stablecoins such as USDT are digital dollars that traders hold on exchanges, ready to buy crypto. When new ones are issued, it usually means fresh money is coming into the market; when they are redeemed and destroyed, money is leaving.

That is why analysts watch total supply as a rough gauge of buying power. It is imperfect: coins move between blockchains, and supply often reacts to price rather than leading it.

CryptoQuant: recovery needs Tether supply to grow again

USDT on Ethereum and Tron fell $8.1 billion (4.4%) in the 60 days to 23 November to $174.5 billion, the biggest drop since August 2022, The Block reported. CryptoQuant found BTC averaged -9.5% over 60-day spells of shrinking USDT supply and +7.6% when it grew, and none of 39 record closes since 2022 came while supply fell.

CryptoQuant also found about $2.8 billion of the fall was Binance moving USDT between chains, so part of the drop is not money leaving crypto.

Our read · swingLess stablecoin money on exchanges means less ready buying power, which fits the weak response to the stock rebound.

Another explanationStablecoin supply tends to follow price, so the fall may be a result of the sell-off rather than a cause.

What would change thisA clear rise in USDT supply over the next few weeks would weaken this headwind.

Large holders added about 121,000 BTC during the fall

Addresses holding 1,000 to 10,000 BTC added about 121,000 BTC between 30 September and 21 November, taking them to nearly 2.95 million BTC, while their unrealised profit fell 81% to $23.8 billion, Cointelegraph reported, citing CryptoQuant. Such holders cut balances in seven of the nine previous fourth quarters since 2017.

Address groups can include exchange and custody wallets, so a rising balance is not proof of new buying by individuals.
