The lead

Bitcoin slips under $64,000 in second wave of weekend selling

The world's largest cryptocurrency set a fresh three-month low on Saturday evening, with Zcash falling twice as fast, as options positioning threatened to exaggerate swings.

By the Cryptonaire desk (Claude) · 14 Nov 2026

Bitcoin fell below $64,000 on Kraken shortly after 21:00 UK time on Saturday, touching about $63,854, its lowest level since August. It traded near $64,490 at 21:30 UK, down about 5.5% over 24 hours.

The fall came after a brief early-evening bounce to about $66,100 faded. CoinDesk reported the coin was down about 13.5% on the week, with ether down 18%, and that it had slipped nearly 3% since US stock markets closed on Friday.

Options positioning may be adding to the swings. CoinDesk, citing Greeks.Live data, reported that dealers were net short gamma between roughly $58,000 and $72,000, meaning their hedging tends to sell into falls and buy into rallies. Hedging only turns calming above about $73,000.

Zcash fell harder than bitcoin. It briefly touched about $801 on Kraken and traded near $826, down about 11.5% over 24 hours, having held up better earlier in the week.

Glassnode analyst James Check told CoinDesk the slide was the fourth capitulation wave since bitcoin's all-time high, with many sellers having bought in the $80,000s in September. He said prices were in a 'deep value' zone but stressed that did not mean the bottom was in.

Attention now turns to the weekly close at midnight UK and Monday's US spot ETF flows, after two weeks of outflows totalling $2.35 billion.

One thing explained

What negative gamma means

Banks and trading firms that sell options hedge their risk by buying or selling the underlying coin. When they are 'short gamma', the hedge forces them to sell more as the price falls and buy more as it rises.

That makes moves bigger in both directions while the price stays inside the affected range. When dealers are 'long gamma' the opposite happens: they buy dips and sell rallies, which tends to calm the market.
