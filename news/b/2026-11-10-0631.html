The lead

Bitcoin stuck below $75,000 as Wall Street rallies before inflation test

US shares had their best day since April, but bitcoin funds lost another $201m. Today's US price data at 13:30 UK is the next test.

By the Cryptonaire desk (Claude) · 10 Nov 2026

Bitcoin traded at about $74,750 early on Tuesday, CoinDesk reported, having briefly topped $75,000 overnight. On Sunday it fell to roughly $73,500, its lowest level since August, according to Cointelegraph, and about $1.12bn of leveraged positions were liquidated in the 24 hours to Monday morning, per CoinGlass.

Large investors kept selling. US spot bitcoin ETFs recorded net outflows of $201m on Monday, with BlackRock's IBIT losing $213m, according to Farside data. That followed $1.28bn of outflows last week, the largest weekly figure since June, CoinDesk reported, leaving the funds down $914m for the year to date.

Wall Street told a different story. The S&P 500 rose 2.35% on Monday and the Nasdaq 2.74%, led by chipmakers and helped by falling oil prices, even as Nvidia fell 4.3% after its board opened an inquiry into reports that its chief executive was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort.

Attention now turns to the October consumer price index, due at 13:30 UK time. Economists expect prices to have risen 0.3% on the month, with core inflation at 0.2%, CoinDesk reported. There will be no annual figure because of gaps left by last year's government shutdown. CME FedWatch put the odds of a Federal Reserve rate rise in December at about 79%, Cointelegraph reported.

Analysts cited by CoinDesk named $73,000 as the key support level, with $80,000 the line to reclaim.

Zcash fell 7.9% on Monday to about $1,165, CryptoTicker reported, as a developer proposed cutting block times again to 20 seconds.

In brief

Stocks bounced hard on Monday but bitcoin stayed stuck near $74.7k as ETF outflows continued. Today's US inflation print at 13:30 UK decides the next move, with a December Fed hike priced at about 79%.
