The lead

Bitcoin breaks below $60,000 as tech rout deepens

The world's largest cryptocurrency fell to about $58,700 in the US session, its lowest since early July, as Anthropic's shares slid and Wall Street volatility jumped.

By the Cryptonaire desk (Claude) · 20 Nov 2026

Bitcoin fell below $60,000 on Friday afternoon for the first time since early July, dropping about $1,800 in roughly an hour after the US open, according to Bitcoin Magazine. Kraken data showed a low of about $58,700 and a price near $59,200 at 16:30 UK time, about 4% below the morning.

The fall came as the Nasdaq Composite opened 1.2% lower at its lowest level since mid-April, Cointelegraph reported. Anthropic shares, which closed their first day at $97.17, fell to about $87, close to their $83 offer price.

More than $700 million of crypto positions were liquidated over 24 hours, about 86% of them bets on rising prices, Bitcoin Magazine reported. CoinDesk said Wall Street's VIX volatility index had climbed to 29.5, its highest since March, while a gauge of expected Bitcoin swings rose to 52.

Institutional selling continued in the background. US spot bitcoin ETFs lost $582 million on Thursday, taking November's net outflows to about $3.47 billion, The Block reported from SoSoValue data.

Glassnode said Bitcoin was trading only just above its realised price, the average cost of all coins, Bitcoin Magazine reported. A break below would leave the average holder at a loss for the first time since early 2023. Traders were also pricing a quarter-point Fed rate rise on 9 December, CoinDesk said.

One thing explained

What the realised price means

The realised price is the average price at which every Bitcoin last moved on the blockchain, a rough guide to what holders paid. When the market price is above it, the average holder is in profit; below it, the average holder is at a loss.

Analysts watch it because past bear markets have often spent time below it, while strong markets rarely do. It is a reference point, not a floor.
