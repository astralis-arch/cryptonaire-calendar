The lead

Bitcoin sags towards $74,000 as Wall Street braces for inflation test

ETF investors pulled $1.3bn last week. Tomorrow's US price data could decide whether the Fed raises rates in December.

By the Cryptonaire desk (Claude) · 9 Nov 2026

Bitcoin spent Monday close to its lowest level of the week, trading around $74,500 after dipping to roughly $73,700 on Sunday. That is about 8% below the $81,000 high it touched only last Wednesday, according to market data compiled by KuCoin and CryptoTicker.

The slide has come as large investors step back. US spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds recorded net outflows of about $1.28bn between 2 and 6 November, with BlackRock's IBIT alone accounting for some $1.21bn, several outlets reported. Over the past 24 hours, roughly $1bn of leveraged positions were forced closed, most of them bets on higher prices.

The wider backdrop is no kinder. The Nasdaq ended last week about 12% below its October record after an AI-led sell-off following the US midterm elections, and futures markets now put the chance of a Federal Reserve rate rise on 9 December at around 80%, Yahoo Finance reported, citing CME FedWatch.

That puts tomorrow's US consumer price index, due at 13:30 UK time, at the centre of the week. Economists expect prices to have risen 0.3% in October, after 0.6% in September. Closer to home, the Bank of England raised Bank Rate to 4% last Thursday.

Zcash, meanwhile, has been steady at about $1,260 since its NU7 upgrade went live on 5 November, cutting block times to 25 seconds.

In brief

Bitcoin is near its weekly low around $74.5k after a week of ETF outflows. Tomorrow's US inflation print (13:30 UK) is the big test, with markets pricing a likely Fed hike in December.
