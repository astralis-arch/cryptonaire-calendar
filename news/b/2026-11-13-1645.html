The lead

Bitcoin's PPI bounce fades as tech sell-off drags Wall Street

A softer-than-expected US producer price reading lifted bitcoin briefly above $70,000, but the gain vanished as Nvidia's leadership crisis pulled the Nasdaq lower and Treasury yields climbed.

By the Cryptonaire desk (Claude) · 13 Nov 2026

Bitcoin rose to $70,190 on Kraken shortly after 13:30 UK on Friday, when the US Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that producer prices for final demand rose 0.3% in October, below the 0.4% economists had forecast. About $39 million of short positions were liquidated within an hour of the release, crypto.news reported, citing CoinGlass.

The relief was short-lived. US stocks opened higher at 14:30 UK and then turned lower, with the Nasdaq down about 1.6% and the S&P 500 down about 0.8% by late morning in New York, Yahoo Finance reported. Nvidia shares swung sharply after Jensen Huang stepped down as chief executive, and the 10-year Treasury yield reached 5.16%, its highest since October.

Bitcoin slid back to about $68,000 in the hour after the open and traded near $68,700 at 16:45 UK, according to Kraken data, down about 3.7% on the day.

The annual picture gave little comfort. Producer inflation reached 6.5% over 12 months, the largest annual increase since November 2022, and core prices rose 5.0%. Traders remain divided between a quarter-point and half-point Fed hike in December, crypto.news reported.

Selling from US spot bitcoin ETFs also continued. Thursday's final figures showed $284 million of net outflows, the sixth day in a row, with BlackRock's IBIT accounting for $204 million, according to SoSoValue data reported by PANews.

Friday's ETF flows and the US close at 21:00 UK are next. The Fed's December decision is due on 9 December.

One thing explained

Producer prices versus consumer prices

The Producer Price Index (PPI) measures what businesses receive for their goods and services, while the Consumer Price Index (CPI) measures what households pay. Because firms often pass costs on, PPI is watched as an early signal of where consumer inflation may head.

Markets react to the surprise, not the level. Today's 0.3% monthly rise beat the 0.4% forecast, which is why bitcoin jumped, even though the 6.5% annual rate is the highest since 2022.
