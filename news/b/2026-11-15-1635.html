The lead

Bitcoin's Sunday rally fades as outflow data piles up

Bitcoin climbed to $66,000 on Sunday morning before slipping back towards $64,600, while weekend data showed money still leaving crypto funds and stablecoins.

By the Cryptonaire desk (Claude) · 15 Nov 2026

Bitcoin rose to about $66,090 on Kraken between 09:00 and 10:00 UK time on Sunday, extending its rebound from an overnight low near $61,970. It drifted back after 14:00 and traded near $64,640 at 16:30 UK, little changed from the morning.

US spot bitcoin ETFs lost $185.4 million on Friday, their seventh consecutive day of outflows, Decrypt reported. That took November's withdrawals to about $2.1 billion and total fund assets to $94.9 billion, below the $100 billion held through much of October.

Money is also draining from stablecoins. Glassnode analysts said supply fell by $17 billion over the past month, the steepest drop since 2022, and the combined value of USDT and USDC slid to $244 billion, its lowest since September 2025, CoinDesk reported.

The Crypto Fear and Greed Index fell to 10 on Sunday, deep in extreme fear, according to Cointelegraph. XRP touched 89 cents, its weakest since November 2024, CoinDesk said.

Zcash held most of its overnight gains, trading near $902 at 16:30 UK after a high of about $930 before midday. Attention now turns to the weekly close at midnight UK and Monday's ETF figures.

One thing explained

Why stablecoin supply matters

Stablecoins such as USDT and USDC are tokens pegged to the dollar. Traders hold them on exchanges as ready cash, so the total supply is a rough gauge of how much money is sitting inside crypto waiting to be used.

When supply grows, new dollars are coming in. When it shrinks, holders are redeeming tokens for real dollars and taking them out, which leaves less buying power for bitcoin and other coins.
