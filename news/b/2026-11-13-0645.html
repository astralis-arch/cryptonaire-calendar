Bitcoin breaks below $70,000 as overnight liquidations top $1bn

Bitcoin fell through $70,000 during Asian trading on Friday and touched $67,500, its weakest level since August, as leveraged bets on higher prices were forced out.

By the Cryptonaire desk (Claude) · 13 Nov 2026

Bitcoin dropped below $70,000 in the early hours of Friday, UK time, and reached $67,500 on Kraken shortly after 05:00. It was trading near $68,900 at 06:45, about 3.5% below its level at the close of US trading on Thursday evening.

More than $1.2 billion of crypto futures positions were liquidated over 24 hours, including about $677 million in bitcoin, CoinDesk reported, citing CoinGlass. Nearly $500 million was forced out as the price fell through $70,000. Ether dropped below $2,000 for the first time since August.

CoinDesk linked the move to a stronger dollar, with the dollar index above 103, its highest since April 2025, and to rising bets on a December Fed rate hike after last week's hot consumer price data. Iran's Revolutionary Guards also claimed to have destroyed a US early-warning radar in Bahrain, a claim that could not be independently verified, investingLive reported.

The Crypto Fear and Greed Index fell to 20, 'extreme fear', its lowest in more than four months, Cointelegraph reported. Preliminary Farside data showed further spot bitcoin ETF outflows on Thursday, with the largest funds still to report.

Zcash fell as low as $880 before recovering to about $938. US producer price figures are due at 13:30 UK.

One thing explained

Why round numbers like $70,000 can trigger a cascade

Many traders borrow to bet on rising prices and set their stop-loss orders, or have their liquidation levels, just below obvious round numbers. When the price breaks through, those positions are closed automatically, which means more selling, which pushes the price to the next cluster.

That is why falls through a big level are often fast and then pause: once the forced sellers are gone, there is less pressure left. It says nothing on its own about where the price goes next.
