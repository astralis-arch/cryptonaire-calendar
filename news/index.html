The lead

Trump-linked WLFI token halves as reserve wallet sells ether

World Liberty Financial's token fell to a record low and its USD1 stablecoin briefly slipped below a dollar, while bitcoin dipped but held above a key level on a quiet Saturday.

By the Cryptonaire desk (Claude) · 28 Nov 2026

WLFI, the governance token of the Trump family-linked World Liberty Financial, fell more than 50% in 24 hours to a record low near $0.016 before recovering to about $0.020, CoinDesk reported. It is roughly 95% below its September 2025 peak of $0.33.

On-chain trackers EmberCN and Lookonchain, citing Arkham data, said a wallet labelled as World Liberty's strategic reserve sold 12,121 ether for about $20 million in stablecoins, at an average near $1,655 and an estimated loss of about $19 million. USD1, the project's stablecoin, briefly fell to 94 cents before returning to nearly $1, according to CoinDesk.

Tron founder Justin Sun, who has been in legal dispute with World Liberty since April, called on exchanges to delist WLFI and USD1, Cointelegraph reported. World Liberty did not respond to requests for comment from either outlet.

Bitcoin was little moved by the episode. It traded between about $58,890 and $60,500 on Kraken on Saturday and stood near $59,370 at 16:30 UK, keeping the daily low above the $58,525 level Galaxy has flagged. Zcash eased to about $720.

With no US trading or economic data until Monday, attention turns to the weekly close at midnight on Sunday.

One thing explained

What a stablecoin depeg means

A stablecoin aims to trade at exactly $1, backed by cash or short-term government debt. When holders doubt the backing or rush to sell, the market price can slip below $1, a depeg, until arbitrage traders redeem coins with the issuer and close the gap.

A brief dip that recovers, as USD1 did on Saturday, is common under stress. A long depeg on a big stablecoin is more serious, because traders use stablecoins as cash across crypto markets.
