The lead

Stocks claw back losses but bitcoin stays pinned near $68,000

Wall Street recovered much of a midday sell-off by Friday's close, but bitcoin ended the US session about 4% lower, resting on its 200-week average and heading for a sixth weekly fall.

By the Cryptonaire desk (Claude) · 13 Nov 2026

Bitcoin traded near $68,260 on Kraken at 21:35 UK on Friday, about 4.4% below where it started the day, after US stock markets closed well off their lows.

The S&P 500 finished 0.4% lower and the Nasdaq 0.9% lower, while the Dow Jones rose 0.1%, according to 24/7 Wall St. At midday in New York the Nasdaq had been down about 1.6%, Yahoo Finance reported, as Nvidia's leadership turmoil and a 10-year Treasury yield of up to 5.17% weighed on technology shares.

Bitcoin did not share in the late recovery. It is sitting on its 200-week moving average, about $67,993, which it slipped below on Friday for the first time in 11 weeks, Cointelegraph reported. CoinDesk said bitcoin is on course for its sixth weekly loss in a row and is down 11% in November.

CoinDesk also pointed to next week's expected initial public offering of Anthropic, which is seeking to raise up to $64.4 billion on Nasdaq, as one possible reason investors were freeing up cash. That link is a suggestion, not an established cause.

US spot bitcoin ETFs had six straight days of outflows to Thursday. Friday's figures are due overnight. Nvidia reports results on Tuesday, and the Fed's December decision follows on 9 December.

One thing explained

The 200-week moving average

The 200-week moving average is the average closing price over roughly the last four years. Because it smooths out whole market cycles, many traders treat it as a long-term floor: in past bear markets bitcoin has spent time near or below it.

It is a reference point, not a guarantee. Bitcoin traded around this line for months in 2023 and again from June to August this year, so touching it says more about where the long-run trend sits than about what happens next.
