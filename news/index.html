The lead

Zcash briefly breaks below $1,000 as bitcoin clings to $73,000

Zcash fell about 12% in a day, its sharpest drop in weeks, while bitcoin dipped to a near three-month low before closing just above a level traders had marked as critical.

By the Cryptonaire desk (Claude) · 11 Nov 2026

Zcash fell as low as $999.57 on Tuesday, its weakest level since early September, Seeking Alpha and Bitcoin.com News reported. The token was down about 12% over 24 hours and roughly 41% below its 2026 high of $1,695, reached on 26 September.

Seeking Alpha linked the fall to leveraged long positions being forced shut and to continuing redemptions from the Grayscale Zcash Trust, which require the trust to sell or withdraw the coins it holds. The same day, Grayscale launched a $1 million Zcash ecosystem programme, with $400,000 in first grants.

Bitcoin also hit its lowest level since late August. Coinbase data showed a low of $72,643 at 16:38 UTC on Tuesday, Forbes reported, after October's US inflation figures came in hotter than forecast. It recovered to about $73,800 by midnight UTC, according to KuCoin market data.

The pressure came from bond markets as well. The US 10-year Treasury yield rose about 14 basis points to 5.00%, TheStreet reported, and the Nasdaq fell 0.74%. Fed Vice Chair Michelle Bowman said in a speech that inflation progress had stalled and that policy was "not on a preset course".

Selling by large holders added to the mood. Bitcoin miner MARA said it had sold 13,597 bitcoin, about $1.1 billion, since 30 September and planned to sell more, CoinDesk reported.

Traders now look to US producer prices on Friday and the Fed's decision on 9 December.

Corrections: Last night's edition (10 Nov, 19:45) said bitcoin's low was near $73,400. Coinbase data reported by Forbes shows a deeper low of $72,643 at 16:38 UTC.

One thing explained

Why trust redemptions can push a coin's price down

A trust like Grayscale's Zcash fund holds real coins on behalf of its shareholders. When investors redeem shares, the trust has to sell or hand over coins to pay them, which puts those coins onto the market.

If many holders redeem at once, that selling arrives on top of normal trading, and in a smaller market like Zcash it can move the price more than it would for bitcoin.
